FedMSE Arcade

Eight short games that explain one real research paper, FedMSE (Nguyen & Beuran, JAIST, 2025). It is about catching hackers on smart‑home networks without spying on anyone. Read the story, then play the games in order.

Badges
0/8
1

Homes are full of smart devices

Doorbells, baby monitors, cameras, thermostats. Each one talks to the internet all day.

2

Hackers hijack them

Malware like Mirai takes over weak devices and turns them into a botnet, an army of devices that attack websites together.

3

Watching from one place is risky

We could send every home's traffic to one big company to check. But that traffic is private, and copying it all is slow and expensive.

4

FedMSE: learn together, keep raw data at home

Each building's router learns what normal traffic looks like. Raw traffic stays home. What the router learned goes to a head office, which blends the best lessons together.

Cheat sheet

Words you'll meet

IoT (Internet of Things)
Everyday objects connected to the internet: smart bulbs, cameras, doorbells.
Botnet
Many hacked devices controlled by one attacker. Mirai and Gafgyt are two famous ones.
Gateway
The router or hub for one building or area. All that area's device traffic passes through it.
Server (head office)
A central computer that collects what the gateways learned and blends it.
Model / detector
A program that learned from examples and can now make decisions on new data.
Federated learning
Training one shared model across many places without moving anyone's raw data.
Autoencoder
A "copy machine" network: it squeezes data into a few numbers, then rebuilds it.
Anomaly
Something that doesn't fit the normal pattern. Here, likely an attack.
Non‑IID data
When different places see very different data, like one building full of cameras and another full of doorbells. (IID = independent and identically distributed.)
MSE (mean squared error)
A score for mistakes: take each error, square it, average them. Smaller is better.
Centroid
The centre point of a group. Far from the centre of "normal" means suspicious.
AUC
A grade for a detector. 100% means it always ranks attacks as more suspicious than normal traffic. 50% is no better than guessing.
